    METHODOLOGY

    How the severity score works

    Each incident gets a severity rating from 1 to 4 from Central Asia Center analysts. It is also tagged with the country it concerns, the sectors it touches, and the outside actors involved: Russia, China, the United States, and other S7 states.

    1. Rescale each rating to 0–100. 1 becomes 0, 2 becomes 33, 3 becomes 67 and 4 becomes 100.
    2. Average within each sector. For a country and month, the ratings in each of the eight sectors are averaged. An incident tagged with several sectors counts toward each of them.
    3. Weight sectors equally. The country score is the simple average of its sector scores, so each sector carries equal weight. Sectors with no incidents in the period are left out rather than counted as zero.
    4. Filter by actor. An actor's score uses only the incidents that actor is involved in. "All actors" uses every incident.

    Only Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan are scored. Incidents located elsewhere, for example in Russia, China or Azerbaijan, appear on the map and in the feed but do not affect any score.

    Incident severity levels

    1
    Meetings between officials with no outcome or agreement, or reporting that a situation continues unchanged.
    2
    A memorandum of understanding or cooperation agreement is signed, or heads of state meet.
    3
    An agreement with tangible outcomes, such as a financial investment or specific figures, or an event of geopolitical significance: great-power competition, terrorism, a shift in relations.
    4
    An agreement with tangible outcomes of exceptional scale, for example billions invested or financed.

    METHODOLOGY

    How the tracker works

    S7+CAT scores each of the five Central Asian republics every month on a 0–100 scale across four composite measures:

    Russian Influence Index (RII)
    The depth, nature and trajectory of Russian penetration: security ties, Eurasian Economic Union membership, Soviet-legacy institutions, labor migration and the dominance of the Russian language.
    Chinese Influence Index (CII)
    The depth, nature and trajectory of Chinese penetration: Belt and Road infrastructure, debt financing, trade, digital infrastructure and institutional partnerships.
    U.S. Engagement Index (USEI)
    The breadth and depth of cooperation with the United States: private investment, commercial partnerships, educational exchange and technology cooperation.
    S7+ Integration Readiness Index (SIRI)
    Each state's capacity and momentum toward regional cooperation within the S7+ framework: intra-regional connectivity, policy convergence, joint initiatives and institutional development.

    Calculating an index

    1. Analysts log each incident, tag the country, the sectors and the outside actors involved, and rate its severity from 1 to 4.
    2. Severity is rescaled to 0–100: level 1 is 0, level 2 is 33, level 3 is 67 and level 4 is 100.
    3. For each country, month and actor, the rescaled severities are averaged within each of the eight sectors: energy, water, education and culture, AI and digital, transportation, agriculture and industry, finance and business, and defense. An incident tagged with several sectors counts in each. A sector with no incidents scores 0.
    4. Each sector contributes 12.5% of the index: index = sum of (sector score × 12.5%). If an actor has no incidents in a country that month, its index is 0.

    Only Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan are scored. Incidents located elsewhere, for example in Russia, China or Azerbaijan, appear on the map and in the feed but don't count toward any index.

    Incident severity

    Severity describes individual incidents only.

    1
    Meetings between officials with no outcome or agreement, or reporting that a situation continues unchanged.
    2
    A memorandum of understanding or cooperation agreement is signed, or heads of state meet.
    3
    An agreement with tangible outcomes, such as a financial investment or specific figures, or an event of geopolitical significance: great-power competition, terrorism or a shift in relations.
    4
    An agreement with tangible outcomes of exceptional scale, for example billions invested or financed.

    Sources

    Incidents are drawn from national trade and customs data, international financial institutions, Belt and Road and Eurasian Economic Union databases, regional reporting and expert assessment. Every incident links to its source. Country profile sources are listed under each profile.
